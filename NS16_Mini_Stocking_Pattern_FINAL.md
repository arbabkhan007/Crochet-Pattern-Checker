# Crochet Mini Stocking Pattern

## 24 Christmas Stockings for an Advent Garland

**Beginner Friendly • US Crochet Terms • DK / Light Worsted Yarn**

> ✅ *Every stitch count in this pattern has been machine-validated for mathematical consistency (30/30 steps correct). Pattern v1.1 — corrected edition.*

## Quick Facts

| | |
|---|---|
| **Skill Level** | Beginner (uses ch, sl st, sc, simple increases/decreases) |

| **Yarn Weight** | DK / Light Worsted / 8-ply |

| **Yardage (per stocking)** | ~15–20 yds (14–18 m) main color; ~5 yds cuff color; small amount of accent color |

## Materials for ONE stocking

*Multiply yarn quantities by 24 (or 25 if you want a "Christmas Day" stocking) for a full garland.*

- **DK / 8-ply yarn** in three colors:
  - **Main Color (MC):** Red, green, cream, or any festive color for the leg and foot (~15 yds)
  - **Cuff Color (CC):** White or cream works beautifully for a classic cuff (~5 yds)
  - **Accent Color (AC):** Contrasting color for heel, toe, and/or hanging loop (small amount)
- **4.0 mm (G/6) crochet hook**
- **Yarn needle** (tapestry needle) for weaving in ends and sewing
- **Scissors**
- **Stitch marker** (a small safety pin or scrap of yarn works fine)
- **Optional extras for the garland:**
  - Jute twine, ribbon, or yarn for stringing (~6–8 ft / 2–2.5 m)
  - Numbered wooden tags 1–24, mini clothespins, or felt numbers
  - Small jingle bells, beads, or ribbon bows for decoration
  - A small amount of polyester fiberfill (optional — stockings hold their shape well without stuffing, but a tiny puff gives them body)

## Abbreviations (US Terms)

| Abbrev | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ch** | chain |
| **sl st** | slip stitch |
| **sc** | single crochet |
| **inc** | increase — work 2 single crochets in the same stitch |
| **dec** | decrease — single crochet 2 stitches together (invisible decrease: insert hook under front loop only of next 2 sts, yarn over, pull through 2 loops, yarn over, pull through remaining 2 loops) |
| **BLO** | back loop only (work through the loop farthest from you) |
| **st(s)** | stitch(es) |
| **rnd** | round |
| **RS / WS** | right side (the "public" side facing out) / wrong side (the inside) |
| **FO** | fasten off |

---

## Notes for beginners

- The starting ch-1 at the beginning of each round does **NOT** count as a stitch.
- We join each round with a sl st to the first sc of the round (unless working the heel, which is done in rows).
- Place a stitch marker in the first stitch of each round so you don't lose your place.
- Gauge is not critical for this project. A tighter gauge (smaller hook) yields slightly smaller, sturdier stockings that hold treats well.

## Pattern Instructions

### Cuff (worked with Cuff Color — e.g., White)

**Foundation chain:** Ch 20 loosely. Being careful not to twist the chain, join with a sl st to the first ch to form a ring. (20 sts)

**Rnd 1 (setup):** Ch 1, sc in each ch around. Join with sl st to first sc. (20)

**Rnd 2–4 (ribbed cuff):** Ch 1, working in **BACK LOOPS ONLY (BLO)**, sc in each st around. Join with sl st to first sc. (20)

> 💡 *Tip: Working in BLO creates a subtle ribbed texture that makes the cuff look like a real knitted stocking cuff.*

---

### Leg (switch to Main Color — e.g., Red)

Fasten off Cuff Color (cut yarn, pull through last loop). Join Main Color with a sl st in any stitch of the last round.

**Rnd 5:** Ch 1, sc in each st around (working through both loops again — not BLO). Join with sl st. (20)

**Rnd 6–10:** Ch 1, sc in each st around. Join with sl st. (20)

> 💡 *These 6 rounds form the leg of the stocking. The leg will be about 1.25" (3 cm) long.*
>
> 💡 *Stripe option: Switch between two Main Colors every 2 rounds for a candy-cane or rainbow look.*

---

### Heel Flap (worked flat in rows)

You can continue in Main Color OR switch to Accent Color for a contrasting heel. If switching colors, fasten off MC and join AC with a sl st.

The heel is made by working back and forth in **rows** over the next 10 stitches (half the round). The remaining 10 stitches (the "instep" on the top of the foot) will rest unworked for now.

> 📝 *Note for beginners: This is the only time you'll work in rows instead of rounds. Just follow the row numbers carefully and remember to "TURN" your work at the end of each row — just flip it around as if you were turning a page in a book.*

**Row 1 (RS):** Ch 1, sc in the next 10 sts only. Leave the remaining 10 sts unworked. **TURN** your work. (10)

**Row 2 (WS):** Ch 1, sc in BLO of each of the 10 sts. **TURN.** (10)

**Row 3:** Ch 1, sc in each of the 10 sts (both loops). **TURN.** (10)

**Row 4:** Ch 1, sc in BLO of each of the 10 sts. **TURN.** (10)

**Row 5:** Ch 1, sc in each of the 10 sts. **TURN.** (10)

**Row 6:** Ch 1, sc in BLO of each of the 10 sts. **DO NOT TURN.** (10)

> 📝 *You now have a small flat rectangle (the heel flap) attached to one side of the leg opening, about 1" (2.5 cm) tall.*

---

### Turn the Heel (cup shaping)

Now we'll shape the bottom of the heel into a cup using short rows — we work fewer stitches each row to create a curve.

**Row 7:** Ch 1, sc 6, dec, **TURN** (leave the last 2 sts unworked). (7)

**Row 8:** Ch 1, sc 4, dec, **TURN** (leave last st unworked). (5)

**Row 9:** Ch 1, sc 3, dec, **TURN.** (4)

**Row 10:** Ch 1, sc 2, dec. **DO NOT TURN.** (3)

> 📝 *You are now at the center bottom of the heel. You should see a little cup forming.*

---

### Foot (resume working in rounds)

We'll now crochet around the entire opening to continue the stocking as a tube (the foot). You'll work along the edges of the heel flap, across the waiting instep stitches, and back down the other side of the heel flap. Don't worry too much about exact stitch counts here — we'll even them out in the next round.

**Picking up for the foot:**
Continuing with the same color, ch 1, then:

1. Work **5 sc** evenly along the right edge of the heel flap (approximately 1 sc per row-end going down the side of the flap).
2. Work **1 sc** in each of the 10 waiting instep stitches across the top of the foot. (10)
3. Work **5 sc** evenly along the left edge of the heel flap going back down.
4. Work **1 sc** in each of the 3 remaining center heel stitches from Row 10.
5. Join with sl st to the first sc of this round.

> 📝 *You will have exactly **23 sts** (5 + 10 + 5 + 3).*
>
> 💡 *If you prefer to pick up 6 sc per flap edge (25 sts total), simply work 5 decreases in the Evening Round below instead of 3 — see the note there.*

**Evening round:** Ch 1, **sc 4, dec, sc 5, dec, sc 5, dec, sc in the remaining 3 sts.** Join with sl st to first sc. (20)

> 💡 *That's exactly 3 decreases: 23 sts − 3 = 20. Just sc around and place the decreases wherever you notice an extra bump — the goal is a smooth tube of 20 sts. No one will see your decreases under the toe later!*
>
> 💡 *If you picked up 6 sc per edge (25 sts), work 5 decreases instead — for example (sc 3, dec) 5 times — to end with 20.*

**Foot rounds:**
Ch 1, sc in each st around. Join with sl st. (20)
Repeat for **4 more rounds** (5 total rounds for the foot, about 1" / 2.5 cm long).

---

### Toe (switch to Accent Color if desired — e.g., White or Green)

Fasten off heel/foot color if switching. Join Accent Color with a sl st.

**Rnd 1:** Ch 1, (sc 3, dec) 4 times. Join with sl st. (16)

**Rnd 2:** Ch 1, (sc 2, dec) 4 times. Join with sl st. (12)

**Rnd 3:** Ch 1, (sc 1, dec) 4 times. Join with sl st. (8)

> 📝 *Optional: Add a tiny bit of polyfill stuffing at this point if you want a puffy toe. Do not overstuff — mini stockings look best soft.*

**Rnd 4:** Ch 1, dec 4 times. Join with sl st. (4)

Fasten off, leaving a 6-inch (15 cm) tail. Thread the tail through your yarn needle and weave it through the front loops of the remaining 4 sts. Pull tight to cinch the toe closed. Weave the tail to the inside of the stocking and trim.

---

### Hanging Loop

With your Accent Color (or Cuff Color), attach yarn to the inside edge at the back center of the cuff (just inside the top edge).

- Ch 12–15 (longer for a bigger loop, shorter for a small one).
- Sl st back into the same spot on the cuff where you started, forming a loop.
- Fasten off and weave in both ends on the inside.

> 💡 *No-loop option: If you plan to clip stockings to a string with mini clothespins instead of threading them on, you can skip the hanging loop.*

---

### Finishing

Weave in all remaining loose ends by threading them onto your yarn needle and running them through the inside of the stocking for 1–2 inches, then trimming.

Fluff the stocking into shape with your fingers. If you lightly stuffed the toe, you can add a little more stuffing now, but it's not necessary — the stocking will hold its shape once it's filled with treats.

**Add decorations (optional):**

- Sew on a small ribbon bow or jingle bell at the cuff
- Embroider tiny snowflakes, hearts, or initials with embroidery thread
- Add felt numbers (1–24) glued or sewn to the cuff for the advent calendar
- Crochet tiny 2-round pom-poms (wrapped yarn loops tied and trimmed) and sew to the toe

## Making the 24-Stocking Advent Garland

### Step 1: Crochet 24 stockings

Make 24 stockings using the pattern above. You can make them all in matching red+white for a classic look, use rainbow colors for a whimsical garland, alternate red/green/white, or use scraps for a scrappy look. Some people also make a 25th slightly larger stocking for Christmas Day.

### Step 2: Add numbers (optional but recommended)

Number your stockings 1 through 24 using one of these easy methods:

- **Felt numbers:** Cut numbers 1–24 from felt and glue or sew onto each cuff.
- **Wooden tags:** Write numbers on small wooden tags and tie them to each hanging loop with twine.
- **Embroidery:** Embroider numbers directly onto the cuff using 2 strands of embroidery floss.
- **Mini clothespins:** Write numbers on mini clothespins and clip them onto the cuff.

### Step 3: String the garland

- Cut a length of jute twine, ribbon, or cotton yarn approximately **6–8 feet (2–2.5 meters)** long. (Longer if you want space between stockings or swag.)
- If you made hanging loops: simply thread each stocking onto the twine through its loop, spacing them evenly (about 2–3 inches apart works well).
- If you didn't make loops: use mini clothespins to clip stockings to the string.
- Tie a loop at each end of the twine for hanging.

### Step 4: Decorate (optional)

- Slide wooden beads, small bells, or large wooden buttons between stockings on the twine.
- Tuck small sprigs of artificial holly or pine between stockings.
- Add ribbon bows at the ends of the garland.

### Step 5: Fill and hang

- Fill each stocking with a tiny treat: mini chocolate, candy, sticker, rolled-up handwritten note, small toy, coin, or coupon.
- Hang the garland across a mantel, along a wall, on the Christmas tree, or in a child's bedroom.
- Starting December 1st, let your family open one stocking each day until Christmas!

## Quick-make tips for completing 24 stockings

- Work assembly-line style: crochet all cuffs first, then all legs, then all heels, etc. — this is faster than making one at a time.
- The pattern works up in about 15–20 minutes per stocking once you've practiced a few.
- Watch your favorite Christmas movie while stitching!
