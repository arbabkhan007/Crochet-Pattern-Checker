# Momo the Loaf Cat

> **No-sew body  ·  Advanced beginner  ·  2 - 2.5 hours**  —  Design Code NS 06

A cat folded into a perfect loaf. One no-sew body on an oval base: the ears are crocheted onto the head and the tail is worked off the body, so there are no separate components to sew on. Finishing still uses small ear-base tacks and embroidery.

## Safety — read this first

Momo uses two 8 mm plastic safety eyes. If an eye, washer or surrounding stitch fails, it can release a small part: lock the washers firmly from the inside before stuffing and closing, then check each eye and the surrounding fabric by hand. An embroidered-eye version removes those plastic components but does not make the whole item a tested toy.

No finished Momo has been shown by Novality Store to comply with ASTM F963, EN 71 or another toy-safety regime; do not describe one as “baby-safe” or compliant. Before supply, the finished-item maker or seller must determine all applicable classification, assessment, testing, documentation, labelling and traceability duties in every destination market. The ears and tail are worked onto the body, but their yarn ends and the face embroidery must still be secured and buried.

## Materials

- Main yarn: worsted #4, about 10 g. Grey, ginger, cream or black. One 25 g ball is enough for two at the stated gauge.

- Contrast: small amounts of white for the chest and paws, pink for the nose and inner ears.

- Hook: 3.5 mm (US E-4) - tight gauge so stuffing cannot show.

- Eyes: 2 x 8 mm safety eyes, or embroider in black.

- Also needed: polyfill about 10 g; tapestry needle; stitch marker; black embroidery floss.

## Gauge & size

Gauge: about 4.5 mm per stitch and 4.3 mm per round. Finished size about 7.9 cm long, 5.2 cm wide and 4.3 cm tall - a low, wide loaf. The 48-stitch oval base sets the whole size of the cat.

## Abbreviations (US terms)

ch - chain  ·  sc - single crochet

inc - increase (2 sc in one st)

dec - invisible decrease  ·  sl st - slip stitch

BLO - back loop only  ·  FO - fasten off

st(s) - stitch(es)  ·  Rnd(s) - round(s)  ·  (n) - stitch count at round end

Work in a continuous spiral unless a piece says otherwise - no joins, no chain 1 between rounds; move the marker up each round.

## Techniques used, in the order you will meet them

1. **Around a chain (oval base)** - THE step that makes the loaf. Crochet into BOTH sides of a starting chain: across the front, corner increases into the last chain, then back along the opposite side and into the last loop. That turns a chain into a flat oval instead of a strip. Do NOT use a magic-ring circle here - a round base changes the intended oval footprint.

2. **Working in a spiral** - No joins and no chain 1 between rounds; move the marker up each round - there is no join to count from.

3. **Back loop only (BLO)** - Work into the far loop only; the unused loops form a raised ridge - the visible edge where the oval base meets the walls. That ridge is what makes the loaf read as folded rather than moulded.

4. **The invisible decrease** - Front loops only of the next two stitches, yarn over and pull through both, then yarn over and pull through the remaining two. Every dec here is worked this way.

5. **Flat rows (ch 1, turn)** - The ears are not worked in the round: chain 1, turn, and work back along the row. Each row is shorter than the one before, forming the ear point.

6. **Working into body fabric** - The tail starts by pushing the hook through the finished body wall - catch a full stitch securely, avoiding eye posts, washers and embroidery; a single loose loop is a weaker anchor. Secure the starting tail before continuing.

## Instructions

### 1. Body — oval base with a longer facial wall

Choose one narrow curved end as the face and the other as the tail. Lay the base with its long axis front-to-back and mark both end midpoints after R6. Carry physical guide threads upward independently of the drifting spiral start.

Pause after R12: mark the full face, place eyes between R10 and R11 about seven stitch positions centre-to-centre, verify washer clearance, lock washers and embroider the nose/mouth below. Keep the mouth clear of the R7 fold. Add a small chest marking only if it fits; it need not sit below every other feature. Secure all ends inside before stuffing. Stuff progressively through the decreases, then cinch the six R19 front loops and bury the tail. The revised wall height needs physical testing; the inherited 4.3 cm height is not a measured result.

### 2. Surface ears — make two, facing the marked front

Pin two short, mirrored base lines on the front upper surface, with about three body-stitch positions between their inner ends. Each line uses FOUR separate whole-stitch surface anchors, not an assumed five-stitch span around one ring. Start near R15 toward the centre front and angle the outer end toward R13, selecting accessible adjacent anchors. Mark all eight anchors before working; they must sit near the front, not halfway down the side flanks. If that placement is crowded, reposition the marked lines before proceeding.

Row 1: join yarn and sc once under each of the four marked anchors (4); ch 1, turn. Row 2: dec x 2 (2); ch 1, turn. Row 3: dec (1). FO; run the tail down the edge, secure it at the base and bury. Embroider a small pink centre. Mirror the second ear and check both face forward. This diagonal attachment route is a prototype requiring a made sample.

### 3. Tail - worked off the body

Join the main yarn to the back of the body near Rnd 10 at the marked rear narrow end. **Surface Rnd 1:** insert the hook under four adjacent whole body stitches arranged as a tiny square and work 1 sc into each (4); place a marker in the first sc. **Rnds 2-8:** sc in each of the 4 tail stitches (4). Work upward in a tight spiral; do not make a magic ring and do not count the body stitches as extra tail stitches. Do NOT stuff - a thin tail curls around the loaf far better than a filled one. FO with a tail; thread it through the front loops of the four tail-tip stitches, pull closed, then run the tail through the tube and secure it in the body. Secure the starting end at the surface anchor too.

## Finishing & assembly

Complete the face at the after-R12 pause described in the body section. Pin eyes, nose, mouth and any chest marking as one layout before making permanent stitches. Markings are optional; do not crowd them between the eyes and base fold. Place two tucked-paw ovals near the front base edge. After closure, work the ears on the marked front surface and the tail at the opposite narrow end near R10. Secure every surface anchor and end.

## Troubleshooting

- **Too tall or too flat:** check actual stitch/round gauge and filling; the revised five-round facial wall is not a guaranteed silhouette.
- **Ears wrap around the sides:** reposition the surface anchor lines closer to the marked front, rather than counting a long span around R15.
- **Face crowded:** simplify markings or use smaller embroidery; do not force eye washers against a seam.
- **Stuffing shows:** use denser fabric and less filling pressure.
