# Coco the Capybara

> **No plastic eyes  ·  Intermediate  ·  2.5 - 3 hours**  —  Design Code NS 04

A low, round, bottom-heavy capybara with a blunt sewn-on muzzle, plump stuffed legs and a calm embroidered sleeping face. No safety eyes and no small plastic parts.

## Materials

- Main yarn: worsted weight (#4), warm brown, about 16 g (paper estimate at roughly 1.6 g per 100 stitches; weigh your first Coco and adjust). A smooth matte yarn shows the stitch texture best.

- Face yarn: worsted weight (#4), dark brown, about 2 g, for the closed eyes, nose and mouth. Use the same weight as the main yarn.

- Hook: 3.0 mm. The gauge is written for this hook.

- Eyes: none. The sleeping face is embroidered - no safety eyes.

- Also needed: polyester fiber filling about 6 g; yarn needle; stitch marker; pins. Pins matter: the muzzle and ears are pinned before sewing.

## Gauge & size

Gauge: 36 sc at the target width suggests about 52 mm diameter in a circular model; measure after stuffing (4.5 mm per stitch, 4.3 mm per round). Check it on the body after Rnd 6 - a stuffed tube, not a flat swatch. If your 36 sts measure wider than 52 mm, crochet more tightly or go down a hook size; a loose gauge will show stuffing.

Unverified historical size target (not measured for Edition 2): about 10.3 cm (4 in) tall, 5 cm (2 in) wide.

## Abbreviations (US terms)

MR - magic ring  ·  ch - chain

sc - single crochet  ·  inc - increase (2 sc in one st)

invdec - invisible decrease  ·  sl st - slip stitch

st(s) - stitch(es)  ·  Rnd(s) - round(s)

FO - fasten off  ·  (n) - stitch count at round end

Work in a continuous spiral - there is no join and no turning chain between rounds. Keep a marker in the first stitch of every round. Work every stitch through both loops unless a note says otherwise.

## Techniques used, in the order you will meet them

1. **The magic ring** - Every piece begins with a magic ring. Pull the tail tight once the first round is complete.

2. **Working in a spiral** - Keep a stitch marker in the first stitch of every round and move it up. There is no seam and no join to count from.

3. **The three-layer leg join** - Each finished leg has a 6-stitch opening. Flatten it into three matched pairs without twisting. For each of three joining sc, insert the hook through one front leg-edge stitch, its matching back leg-edge stitch and the next body stitch; complete one sc. All six leg stitches are caught exactly once, and three body anchors make three body stitches. Do not increase through a leg.

## Notes

The expanding underside is not a stack of vertical rows. Do not multiply body round numbers by round gauge to calculate leg attachment elevations or total height. Measure the assembled piece; the historical 10.3 cm target is not established.

## Instructions

### 1. Legs — make four identical legs

R1: 6 sc in MR (6). R2: [sc, inc] x 3 (9). R3–R7: sc around (9 each). Stuff the hoof lightly, leaving the top soft. R8: [sc, invdec] x 3 (6). FO with a tail. Flatten each opening into three matched pairs. Equal lengths are the starting prototype, not a guarantee that all feet will meet the table; check attachment angle and filling before finishing.

### 3. Body & head - one piece

Immediately after Rnd 15, embroider both closed eyes as described under Finishing, knotting and burying the floss ends inside while the 36-stitch opening is clear. Then work Rnds 16-20, stuffing the head as directed. Cinch the remaining 6 stitches closed and weave the tail inside the body. Do not decrease the Rnd 10 waist further - a narrow neck cannot hold the head upright on this shape.

Leg map — each joining sc catches both stitches of one flattened leg pair and one body anchor. R4 consumes 3 + 2 + 3 + 10 = 18 old stitches and makes 3 + 3 + 3 + 15 = 24. Its back-leg joins occupy NEW R4 stitches 1–3 and 7–9. R5 consumes 12 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 24 and makes 16 + 3 + 4 + 3 + 4 = 30. Its front legs use OLD R4 stitches 13–15 and 19–21. Thus the four reference centres are 2, 8, 14 and 20: the back pair are adjacent, and the front pair are adjacent, with no shared join anchors.

Mark the physical front midway between the two front legs (above old R4 stitches 16–18), and the back opposite it. Preserve those markers upward rather than treating the spiral start as the face centre. The map fixes adjacency, not physical symmetry after increases and stuffing. Pin/check all feet on a flat surface before proceeding; the intended silhouette is an upright stylized animal, not a horizontal realistic capybara.

## Finishing & assembly

Muzzle: stuff lightly and pin it to the front of the head over Rnds 12-14. Sew three quarters of the perimeter with matching brown, pause to adjust the stuffing, then embroider the nose and mouth on the established curve. Knot those embroidery ends inside the muzzle while the last quarter is still open, finish the seam, and bury the sewing tail inside the body. It should sit proud of the head, not flush. Centring matters - the eyes are 6 stitches apart and a muzzle pinned even slightly off-centre crowds one eye.

Eyes: embroider - do not use safety eyes. Complete them immediately after Body Rnd 15, before the decreases and head stuffing. With dark brown, work a shallow downward arc about 3 stitches wide on each side across Rnds 14-15, 6 stitches apart, with a tiny tick angled down at each outer end. Knot and bury every floss end inside.

Nose & mouth: after three quarters of the stuffed muzzle is sewn on, work a small dark triangle at top centre, a short vertical line down from it, and a soft curved mouth to one side. Knot the ends inside through the open part of the seam, then finish sewing the muzzle.

Ears: pinch the base of each ear so it cups forward, then sew at Rnd 15, about 5 stitches apart, angled slightly outward. Rnd 15 is a full 36-st round, so this puts the ears on top of the head where a capybara's belong.

Legs: the 3-sc joins locked each leg's pinched top into the body round. Check that all six leg-edge stitches are caught in three pairs; if a sliver stays open at the side of a leg top, tack it shut with 2-3 whip stitches through all layers - it sits on the underbody and stays invisible.

Final shaping: roll the finished piece gently between your palms to settle the stuffing into a round, bottom-heavy shape.

Before you sew - lay every component out and check: 4 identical 8-round legs, 2 ears, 1 muzzle, 1 body & head, closed at the crown. The sleeping face is all embroidery - closed-eye arcs, a small triangle nose and a soft curved mouth.

## Troubleshooting

- **Feet do not meet the table:** check the four-anchor map, equal leg lengths, mirrored angles and even filling. Do not assume R4 and R5 are one row-height apart vertically.
- **Leg seam gaps:** every six-stitch rim must be caught as three pairs; secure loose side gaps with the retained tail.
- **Head unstable:** preserve the 32-stitch waist, check filling and do not add extra narrowing.
- **Stuffing shows:** use denser fabric rather than overfilling.

- Join position is counted around the expanding underside, not measured vertically. Validate the stance on a complete sample.

- Bottom-heavy by design: stuff the lower body firmly and keep the shape squat. Coco is meant to be one continuous curve from base to crown - she stands on her feet, not her belly.
