## Glossary & Operations

- `ch` = chain
- `sl st` = slip stitch
- `st(s)` = stitch(es)
- `sc` = single crochet
- `inc` = 2 sc in same st
- `dec` = invisible decrease
- `FLO` / `BLO` = Front Loop Only / Back Loop Only
- `split-sc` = Waistcoat stitch — sc worked directly into the centre "V" of the stitch post
- `inv-flip` = **turn the open edge of a tube inside-out before seaming**, so the fabric's right
  side meets the other edge's wrong side. This is the orientation-reversing join.

> The original glossary defined five terms, three of which (`ch-sp`, `3-st-inc`, `3-st-dec`) were
> never used, and omitted all seven operations the pattern is actually built from. `inv-flip` has
> also been redefined to match how it is actually used — the original defined it as turning the
> *work* inside-out through a ring, which is a different manoeuvre.

## Notes — read before starting

1. All three sections are worked in **continuous spirals** unless a round says to join. Mark the
   first stitch of every round.
2. **Nothing is stuffed.** This is a surface, not a solid.
3. Two of the three seams are stated as a stitch count on each side. **Check both numbers before
   you sew** — the original's assembly table asserted three pairings and none of them matched.

## SECTION 2: The Self-Intersecting Inner Core

*Restructured. The flange now starts the piece instead of growing off Round 18 in FLO.*

> **Why.** In the original, Round 19 was worked FLO of Round 18, leaving Round 18's back loops free
> — and the assembly then grafted Section 3 onto those same loops. That puts **three sheets along
> one circle** (tunnel below, flange above via the front loops, Section 3 via the back loops). A
> Klein bottle is a 2-manifold: exactly two sheets meet everywhere. Moving the flange to Round 1
> gives the tunnel two clean boundaries and no branch line.

**Round 1 (Flange Edge):** With Color B, **ch 32**, sl st to first ch to form an open ring. (32 sts)

> 32 — matching the port's 32 boundary positions exactly.

**Round 2:** [sc 2, dec] 8 times. (24 sts)

**Round 3:** [sc 1, dec] 8 times. (16 sts)

**Rounds 4–18:** sc in each st around. (16 sts)

Fasten off Color B, leaving a 16-inch tail. **Leave both ends open** — Round 1 (32 sts) is the
flange that seals the port, Round 18 (16 sts) is the tunnel end that meets Section 3.

## SECTION 4: Inversion Loop & Self-Intersection Assembly

```
+-----------------------+------------------------+------------------------------+
| Source Edge           | Target Edge            | Seam Parity & Orientation    |
+-----------------------+------------------------+------------------------------+
| Sec 2 R1 Flange       | Sec 1 R8 port boundary | 32 sts <---> 32 sts (Normal) |
| Sec 3 R42 Neck End    | Sec 2 R18 Tunnel End   | 16 sts <---> 16 sts (FLIPPED)|
+-----------------------+------------------------+------------------------------+
```

> Two rows, both verified against the rounds that produce them. The original's table had three rows
> and none of them matched: it claimed a 48-stitch edge where Section 1 has 16, a 16-stitch edge
> where Section 1 has a closed 8-stitch magic ring, and an 18-stitch edge where Section 2 has 16.

### Boundary closure

| boundary | resolution |
|---|---|
| Sec 1 Round 1 | closed magic-ring cap — no boundary ✔ |
| Sec 1 Round 8 port (32) | ↔ Sec 2 Round 1 flange (32) ✔ |
| Sec 3 Round 42 (16) | ↔ Sec 2 Round 18 (16), with the inv-flip ✔ |

No free boundary remains, and **exactly two sheets meet at every seam.**

### Directives

1. **Pass-Through Insertion.** Push Section 2 through the Round 8 port from the inside of Section 1
   outward, flange first, until the flange sits flat against the outer wall and the tunnel's far end
   protrudes. A 16-stitch slit is 2.29 in long and opens to a hole of ~4.57 in perimeter; the shaft
   is 2.29 in around, so it passes through without forcing.
2. **Flange Seaming.** Whipstitch Section 2's Round 1 (32 sts) to the port's 32 boundary positions —
   16 into the skipped Round 7 stitches and 16 into the Round 8 chain stitches (32 ↔ 32), one
   stitch for one stitch.
3. **Neck Loop Traversal.** Guide the open end of Section 3 through the centre of Section 2's tunnel
   until it emerges.
4. **Non-Orientable Inversion Grafting.**
   - **inv-flip:** turn the open edge of Section 3 Round 42 inside-out, so its *wrong* side will
     face Section 2's *right* side. **This reversal is the whole point** — without it the result is
     an ordinary torus.
   - Align Section 3 Round 42 (16 sts) with Section 2 Round 18 (16 sts).
   - Join with `split-sc` through the inner posts so the fabric transitions from the outer face to
     the inner face without a visible ridge.

### Why this one is genuinely non-orientable

Section 1 plus Section 3 form a sphere (the capped bulb) drawn out into a long neck. Section 2 is a
tube whose two ends attach to that sphere — at the port and at the neck's end. A tube joining a
sphere to itself is a **handle**; a handle attached with one end **reversed** is a **cross-handle**.

**Sphere + one cross-handle = Klein bottle.** The `inv-flip` in step 4 is what makes it a cross-
handle rather than an ordinary one, and the absence of any branch line is what makes the result a
manifold at all.
