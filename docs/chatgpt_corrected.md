## Pattern 1: Classic Amigurumi Bear — Corrected

1. Head & Body

**Round 1:** Magic ring, 6 sc. **(6 sts)**
**Round 2:** `[inc]` around. **(12 sts)**
**Round 3:** `[sc 1, inc]` × 6. **(18 sts)**
**Round 4:** `[sc 2, inc]` × 6. **(24 sts)**
**Rounds 5–8:** sc around. **(24 sts)**
**Round 9:** `[sc 2, dec]` × 6. **(18 sts)**

Insert safety eyes between R6–R7, approximately 4 stitches apart.

**Round 10:** `[sc 1, dec]` × 6. **(12 sts)**
Stuff the head lightly.

Corrected neck transition

**Round 11:** `[sc 1, inc]` × 6. **(18 sts)**
**Round 12:** `[sc 2, inc]` × 6. **(24 sts)**

This fixes the original 12 → 24 jump. The intermediate 18-stitch round prevents the abrupt expansion.

**Rounds 13–15:** sc around. **(24 sts)**
**Round 16:** `[sc 2, dec]` × 6. **(18 sts)**
**Round 17:** `[sc 1, dec]` × 6. **(12 sts)**
**Round 18:** `[dec]` × 6. **(6 sts)**

Fasten off, stuff if necessary, and cinch the final 6 stitches closed.

2. Arms — Make 2

**Round 1:** Magic ring, 6 sc. **(6 sts)**
**Round 2:** `[sc 2, inc]` × 2. **(8 sts)**
**Rounds 3–8:** sc around. **(8 sts)**
**Round 9:** `[dec]` × 4. **(4 sts)**

Do **not** cinch the opening.

Fasten off, leaving a long sewing tail. Flatten the 4-stitch opening and sew it closed/flat while attaching the arm to the body.

This makes the arm suitable for a flat side attachment instead of creating a bulky closed end.

3. Legs — Make 2

**Round 1:** Magic ring, 6 sc. **(6 sts)**
**Round 2:** `[inc]` × 6. **(12 sts)**
**Rounds 3–5:** sc around. **(12 sts)**
**Round 6:** `[sc 2, dec]` × 3. **(9 sts)**
**Round 7:** sc around. **(9 sts)**

Stuff lightly.

Fasten off, leaving a sewing tail. Flatten the opening and sew the legs to the lower sides of the body.

4. Ears — Make 2

**Round 1:** Magic ring, 6 sc. **(6 sts)**
**Round 2:** `[inc]` × 6. **(12 sts)**
**Round 3:** `[sc 1, inc]` × 6. **(18 sts)**
**Round 4:** sc around. **(18 sts)**

Fasten off, leaving a sewing tail. Do not stuff. Flatten the ear and sew it to the sides/top of the head.

5. Assembly

1. Sew the legs to the lower portion of the body.
2. Sew the arms to the sides.
3. Sew the ears to the head.
4. Check that the safety eyes are securely locked from the inside.
5. Weave in all remaining ends.

Corrections made

- **Defect 1:** Fixed with an 18-stitch transition before returning to 24.
- **Defect 2:** Arm is flattened rather than cinched into an unsuitable end-cap.
- **Defect 3:** Legs and ears are now included.

## Pattern 2: Celestial Wyvern — Corrected

1. Torso & Leg Join

The original joining arithmetic is actually valid **if the chains are subsequently worked into**.

Legs

**Rounds 1–12, Leg 1:** Work 12 sc around. Fasten off, leaving a tail.

**Rounds 1–12, Leg 2:** Work 12 sc around. Keep yarn attached.

Joining Round

**Round 13:**

1. Sc around Leg 2 — **12 sts**
2. Ch 3.
3. Sc around Leg 1 — **12 sts**
4. Sc across the 3-chain bridge — **3 sts**

**Total: 30 sts.**

There is therefore no mathematical “missing 3 body stitches” in this construction. The 3 chain stitches form a crotch bridge; they aren't pre-existing body stitches.

For a cleaner crotch, work the first and last stitches of each bridge tightly and close any tiny corner openings with the yarn tail during assembly.

Round 14

Sc around all 30 stitches.

**(30 sts)**

2. Wing Edging

The original:

> `[sl st, hdc, dc, hdc, sl st]` in every stitch

produces 70 stitches from 14 bases:

14 × 5 = **70**

That arithmetic is correct, but it creates an extremely dense edging.

Corrected edging

**Row 1:** Ch 15, sc in 2nd ch from hook and across. **(14 sts)**

**Row 2:** `[sl st, hdc, sl st]` in each stitch across. **(42 sts)**

This gives a 3-stitch fan per base stitch rather than five stitches per base.

For an even flatter edge, use:

**Alternative Row 2:** `[sc, hdc]` in each stitch across. **(28 sts)**

Corrections made

- The **30-stitch leg join was not actually arithmetically defective**.
- The crotch bridge is now explicitly explained so it isn't mistaken for omitted body stitches.
- The wing edging has been reduced from 5× to 3× density.

## Pattern 3: Clockwork Dragon — Corrected Defect Sections

This pattern cannot be reconstructed completely from the supplied text because several preceding/following rounds are omitted. However, the listed arithmetic errors can be repaired exactly.

1. Head

Short-row rejoin

The original says:

> Round 12: Works 28 of 34 perimeter positions.

The missing 6 positions must be explicitly incorporated.

Use:

**Round 12:** Work across the 28 active stitches, then work across the six short-row/jaw transition stitches to close the perimeter.

**Total: 34 perimeter positions.**

If the six positions are intentionally skipped to create a mouth opening, they must instead be explicitly marked as skipped and closed later. They cannot simply disappear.

Corrected Round 15

The original:

> `[sc 5, dec] × 2, sc 2`

consumes:

- first repeat = 7
- second repeat = 7
- final sc 2 = 2

Total consumed = **16**

It produces:

- 5 sc + 1 decrease = 6
- another 6
- 2 sc

Total = **14 sts**

So the original cannot produce 16 stitches.

If the intended result is 16 sts:

Use:

**Round 15:** `[sc 5, dec] × 2, sc 4. (16 sts)`

This requires 18 starting stitches:

7 + 7 + 4 = **18 consumed**
6 + 6 + 4 = **16 produced**

Therefore the preceding round must contain **18 stitches**.

2. Torso

If the torso arrives at **38 stitches** and the intended decrease is six stitches, a mathematically valid correction is:

**Round 15:** `[sc 4, dec] × 6, sc 2.`

Count:

- six repeats consume 6 × 6 = 36
- final 2 sc = 2
- total consumed = **38**
- each repeat produces 5 stitches
- 6 × 5 + 2 = **32**

Therefore:

**Round 15: 38 → 32 sts**

If the intended starting count is actually 40, then use:

**Round 15:** `[sc 4, dec] × 6, sc 4. (34 sts)`

40 stitches are consumed and 34 remain.

The original should not claim a six-repeat decrease without specifying which of these starting counts is intended.

3. Leg/torso seam

Original:

> Sew 3 unworked leg sts to 4 skipped torso sts.

This is impossible as written.

Corrected assembly

Use one of these constructions consistently:

**Option A:** 3 leg stitches ↔ 3 torso stitches

or

**Option B:** 4 leg stitches ↔ 4 torso stitches

The cleaner repair is **3 ↔ 3**, because it preserves the existing three-stitch leg reference.

4. Wing Row 4

Original:

> `FLO sc 12, inc` on 14 sts.

This consumes only:

12 + 1 = **13 base stitches**

and produces:

12 + 2 = **14 stitches**

Corrected version for a 15-stitch result

**Row 4:** `FLO sc 12, inc, sc 1. (15 sts)`

Consumes:

12 + 1 + 1 = **14 base stitches**

So this requires a 14-stitch starting row, not 15.

If the row truly begins with **14 stitches**, the corrected result is:

**14 → 15**

If the preceding row has **15 stitches**, use:

`FLO sc 13, inc, sc 1`

That consumes 15 and produces 16.

Thus the original's 14/15 counts need to be synchronized with the preceding row.

5. Wing Row 5

Rather than placing `fpdc` directly into a short sc foundation, add a proper structural foundation.

**Recommended correction:**

**Row 5:** Work the fpdc into the designated posts of the preceding structural row, with one post stitch aligned to each corresponding foundation position.

Do not treat a short 14–15 stitch sc edge as though it contains enough evenly spaced post-stitch anchors for a much wider decorative structure.

6. Wing Tab

Original dimensions:

- Tab = 1.17 in
- Socket = 0.55 in

The tab/socket ratio is:

1.17 ÷ 0.55 ≈ **2.13**

Corrected target

For a close-fitting tab, make the tab approximately the same width as the socket, allowing only a small insertion tolerance.

For example:

- Socket: **0.55 in**
- Tab: approximately **0.50–0.55 in**

Do not make the tab 1.17 in wide.

7. Tail Rounds 22–24

Where the original states **16** but produces **14**, the round needs two additional produced stitches.

A generic correction is:

`[sc 5, dec] × 2, sc 4`

This consumes:

7 + 7 + 4 = **18**

and produces:

6 + 6 + 4 = **16**.

Therefore the preceding round must contain 18 stitches.

8. Safety Eyes

Move the safety-eye instruction into the actual head section:

> Insert safety eyes between the designated head rounds before stuffing. Secure the washers from the inside.

This eliminates the unused/“ghost” material listing.

## Pattern 4: Abyssal Leviathan — Corrected

1. Primary Tentacles

The original tentacle is indeed closed at both ends.

That is not necessarily inherently wrong for a stuffed tentacle, but it **cannot subsequently be treated as an open socket**.

Corrected construction

If the tentacle is intended to be attached as a closed stuffed appendage:

- Keep the magic ring closed.
- Work the tentacle.
- Stuff lightly.
- Flatten the final opening.
- Sew the flattened tip to the body.

Do **not** instruct the crocheter to insert body stitches into the sealed tentacle.

If an actual socket is intended, leave the base open instead:

> Do not cinch the base. Keep the opening available for joining to the body.

The pattern must choose one construction.

2. Body Hub

The original Round 15 actually produces **48 stitches**, not 54.

Let's count:

- Body 4
- Tentacle 4
- Body 4
- Arm 3
- Arm 3
- Body 12
- Arm 3
- Arm 3
- Body 4
- Tentacle 4
- Body 4

Total:

**4 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 12 + 3 + 3 + 4 + 4 + 4 = 48**

So the corrected round is:

Round 15

`sc 4 Body, sc 4 Tentacle 1, sc 4 Body, sc 3 Arm 1, sc 3 Arm 2, sc 12 Body, sc 3 Arm 3, sc 3 Arm 4, sc 4 Body, sc 4 Tentacle 2, sc 4 Body.`

**(48 sts)**

The simplest repair is to change the stated count from 54 to **48**, rather than inventing six additional stitches.

Round 16

Original:

> sc in all 54 sts

Corrected

**Round 16:** sc around all 48 stitches. **(48 sts)**

Do not work into additional “outer limb” stitches unless those stitches have explicitly been created as part of the round.

Round 17

The original six-repeat formula:

`[sc 7, dec] × 6`

requires 54 stitches.

Since the corrected R16 has 48 stitches, use:

**Round 17:** `[sc 6, dec] × 6. (42 sts)`

Each repeat consumes 8 stitches and produces 7.

6 × 8 = **48 consumed**
6 × 7 = **42 produced**

Assembly 4

Original:

> 5 secondary-arm stitches to 3 body socket stitches.

Corrected

Use matching seam counts:

> Sew 3 secondary-arm stitches to the corresponding 3 body socket stitches.

If five arm stitches are structurally required, the body socket must also be five stitches wide.

3. Cranial Vault

R15

Original:

> `Switch to Color B in FLO, [sc 5, bpdc 1] × 7.`

The `bpdc` creates a ridge toward the inside/back side of the work depending on construction and orientation.

Corrected decorative ridge

Use a **front-facing post stitch** instead:

`Switch to Color B in FLO, [sc 5, fpdc 1] × 7.`

However, the foundation must have suitable post positions. Do not use post stitches on an unsuitable short foundation row merely because the stitch is available in the glossary.

Cranial R19

Original:

> `[sc 5, dec] × 6. (36 sts)` on 44 sts.

Six repeats consume:

6 × 7 = **42 stitches**

leaving **2 stitches unworked**.

Correct 44 → 36 decrease

Use:

**Round 19:** `[sc 3, dec] × 8, sc 4. (36 sts)`

Count:

- 8 × 5 = 40 consumed
- 4 = 44 consumed
- 8 × 4 + 4 = **36 produced**

No stitches are left unworked.

4. Fin

Row 4

Original:

> `FLO sc 15, inc. (18 sts)` on 17 sts.

This consumes only 16 base stitches and produces 17.

Corrected

**Row 4:** `FLO sc 15, inc, sc 1. (18 sts)`

Consumes:

15 + 1 + 1 = **17**

Produces:

15 + 2 + 1 = **18**

Exactly correct.

Fin Row 6

The stated “5 stitches per row end” creates excessive edging density.

Corrected edging

Use **2–3 stitches per row end** instead of 5.

For example:

> Work `[sc, hdc]` evenly across the row ends.

If a decorative scallop is wanted:

> `[sl st, hdc, sl st]` across each selected row-end group.

The exact number should be based on the number of physical row ends rather than multiplying every row end by five.

Assembly 3

Original:

- Fin = 3.33 in
- Body seam = 1.69 in

These dimensions are incompatible for a direct edge-to-edge attachment.

Corrected construction

Either:

1. Reduce the fin's attachment edge to approximately the body's 1.69-in seam, **or**
2. Increase the body attachment area to approximately the fin's attachment length, **or**
3. Gather the fin edge deliberately and state that gathering is required.

Do not instruct a flat 3.33-in edge to be sewn directly to a 1.69-in edge without a gathering instruction.

## Pattern 5: Void-Warped Chimera — Corrected

1. Cranial Core & Torso

These three decrease rounds have straightforward arithmetic fixes.

Round 15

Starting with 44:

`[sc 5, dec] × 6, sc 2. (38 sts)`

Consumes:

6 × 7 + 2 = **44**

Produces:

6 × 6 + 2 = **38**

Correct.

Round 16

The original:

`[sc 2, fptr 1 into Round 14] × 12`

consumes only:

12 × 3 = **36**

leaving 2 stitches from the 38-stitch round.

Corrected count

**Round 16:** `[sc 2, fptr 1 into Round 14] × 12, sc 2. (38 sts)`

Now all 38 stitches are consumed and 38 stitches are produced.

However, the structural problem remains: an `fptr` reaching from R16 down to R14 spans two rounds. If a strong raised texture rather than a long crossing effect is intended, anchor the post stitch into **Round 15** instead.

Round 17

Starting with 38:

`[sc 4, dec] × 6, sc 2. (32 sts)`

Consumes:

6 × 6 + 2 = **38**

Produces:

6 × 5 + 2 = **32**

No stitches are dropped.

Round 18

Starting with 32:

`[sc 3, dec] × 6, sc 2. (26 sts)`

Consumes:

6 × 5 + 2 = **32**

Produces:

6 × 4 + 2 = **26**

Again, all stitches are accounted for.

2. Multi-Limb Torso

The tentacle/socket problem needs the same construction decision as Pattern 4.

If the tentacle is closed:

> Attach the tentacle externally with a sewing seam.

If it is intended to form a socket:

> Leave the joining end open and work the torso/socket stitches directly into that opening.

You cannot simultaneously cinch the tentacle closed and later work into its opening.

3. Frill

The original claim:

> 756 stitches on 108 base stitches = 21× circumference

is mathematically incorrect.

756 ÷ 108 = **7**

So the actual density is **7 stitches per base stitch**, not 21.

Nevertheless, 7× is still an extremely aggressive frill.

Corrected 3× frill

For 108 base stitches:

108 × 3 = **324 stitches**

Use a three-stitch group such as:

`[sl st, hdc, dc]` **in each base stitch around.**

Result:

**108 → 324 stitches**

This produces a substantial decorative frill without the original 7× explosion.

4. Assembly

Directive 1

Original:

> Graft 30-stitch partition into 38-stitch cranial R15.

These don't match.

Clean correction

Make the partition **36 stitches** and make the cranial attachment edge **36 stitches**.

For example, if Cranial R15 starts with 44 stitches, use:

`[sc 3, dec] × 8, sc 4`

Result:

44 → **36 stitches**

Then make the partition finish at **36 stitches**.

This gives a direct 36 ↔ 36 graft.

Directive 2

Original:

> Head base 26 sts → torso top 18 sts.

These cannot be joined flat without gathering.

Preferred correction

Change the torso-top transition to **26 stitches** so the assembly becomes:

**26 head stitches ↔ 26 torso stitches**

If the torso must remain 18 stitches, explicitly instruct:

> Gather the 26-stitch head base evenly around the 18-stitch torso opening.

Without that instruction, the seam count is contradictory.

Directive 3

Original:

> Cinch 6 held tentacle sts to 6 skipped body sts.

But R7 works 21 of 24 body stitches, leaving only:

24 − 21 = **3 stitches**

Corrected directive

Either:

> Cinch the **3 held tentacle stitches** to the **3 skipped body stitches**.

or change R7 so that six body stitches are intentionally skipped.

The first option is the smallest correction.

Directive 4 — Safety Eyes

Safety eyes should **not** be mounted on a free-hanging treble/frill edge.

Corrected instruction

Move the eyes to the structural cranial/head fabric:

> Insert the two safety eyes through the reinforced cranial fabric before stuffing. Secure each eye with its washer from the inside.

The frill should remain decorative and should not be used as the mounting substrate.
